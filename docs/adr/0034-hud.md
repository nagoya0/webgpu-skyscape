# 34. A HUD in two layers, after the F-16C's

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-07
- Amended: 2026-10-08 (the placeholder path and the Tokyo area removed, [ADR 0036](0036-remove-the-tokyo-area.md))

## Context

The maintainer wanted a head-up display before the JSBSim flight path, so that the path's
manoeuvres are easier to follow (speed, height, heading and attitude on screen). ADR 0018 sets an
F-16-class aircraft. The F-16's HUD differs between versions, and pictures found on the web rarely
say which version or simulator they show.

The demo's camera has a 70° vertical field of view; the real HUD's field of view is 25° across.
Drawn at the real HUD's angles, the symbols are small on screen.

## Decision

- **Two layers.**
  - *The aircraft's HUD*: everything a real HUD would show. It moves with the airframe's
    vibration, so the shake in turns and in clouds moves it with the scene, and symbols that mark
    the scene stay on it.
  - *Fixed to the screen*: what is not part of the aircraft. For now the scene's time, top left,
    below it the flight model, and below that where the aircraft is flying over.
- **Drawn into the image.** Both layers are drawn with Canvas 2D and laid over the image as the
  last step of the post-processing, after the water drops on the screen, so a recording of the
  canvas includes them.
- **Reference**: the HUD symbology of the DCS: F-16C Viper Early Access Guide (Eagle Dynamics),
  an F-16C Block 50. Not all of it: the maintainer chose the elements one at a time. They are
  drawn by this project from the guide's layout and meaning; no images from the guide are used.
- **Look**: text and lines in HUD green with a glow; the typeface Share Tech Mono (SIL Open Font
  License), chosen by the maintainer from eight open typefaces; the HUD semi-transparent over the
  image, and its lines thinner than at first.
- **Size**: elements that do not mark the scene are drawn three times the guide's size, as the
  velocity scale was sized with the maintainer. Elements that mark the scene (the horizon line and
  the attitude bars, the boresight cross, the flight path marker) sit at their true angles.
- **The aircraft's HUD, as chosen with the maintainer:**
  - *Velocity scale*: ground speed in knots, with its letter G. Only the ground speed: the flight
    path has no wind, so it needs no atmosphere model; the real HUD can also show calibrated and
    true airspeed.
  - *Altitude scale*: feet above mean sea level, the velocity scale mirrored about the boresight
    so the two are symmetric. Thousands commas are drawn between the digits without taking a
    character's width, as in the guide.
  - *Heading scale*: magnetic heading, as in the real aircraft; the true heading less the area's
    magnetic declination. Placed so that its lubber line's top is level with the bottoms of the
    velocity and altitude tapes.
  - *Roll indicator*: marks at 0, 10, 20, 30 and 45° of bank and a caret that moves with the bank.
    The caret stops at the 45° mark; the real one also squashes sideways there, which is left out.
  - *Attitude bars*: only the five bars nearest the nose's pitch, every bar canted by half its
    angle into a caret pointing at the horizon, as in the real aircraft. Centred on the nose's
    heading rather than on the flight path marker, as in games.
  - *Boresight cross*, open in the middle, and the *flight path marker*, where the aircraft is
    actually going.
- **Fixed to the screen:**
  - *The scene's time*: "SCENE 2026-10-07 16:30 JST", the time given to the atmosphere, with
    "SCENE" in front so it is not taken for the viewer's own clock.
  - *The flight model*: "FLIGHT MODEL JSBSIM F-16A", between the time and the place (added by the
    maintainer, 2026-10-07): what the flight path is computed with, JSBSim and its F-16 model,
    which its file names an F-16A; in capitals like the other lines. The HUD itself stays after
    the F-16C's.
  - *Where the aircraft is*: "FLYING OVER SUSONO, SHIZUOKA", in romaji, or "FLYING OVER THE SEA".
    The municipality comes from the National Land Numerical Information administrative areas
    (CC BY 4.0, credited on screen), cut to the course's surroundings, with a romaji table made by
    hand; the line changes as the aircraft crosses a boundary. Chosen over GSI's reverse geocoder,
    whose terms for use by other sites are not clear, and which would depend on the network.
- **`?hud=0`** leaves out the aircraft's HUD.

## Consequences

- On the placeholder path the nose always points along the velocity, so the flight path marker
  sits on the boresight cross; the JSBSim path's angle of attack and sideslip will move it.
- To check with the JSBSim path's manoeuvres: the instruments must stay right through inverted
  flight.
- The place line covers the course's surroundings only (within 60 km of the Hakone origin); a
  wider course needs more names in the romaji table.

### Implementation details, not discussed

- The HUD layers are uploaded to the GPU only when drawn; the aircraft layer is drawn in the
  airframe's frame and turned by the camera's offset from it (`src/hud/hud.ts`).
- The opacity is 75 % and the line width 0.1° (`HUD_OPACITY`, `HUD_LINE`).
- The flight path marker's velocity is the difference to where the path is 0.05 s ahead.
- The bank is the roll of the attitude taken as yaw, pitch and roll, so it is undefined with the
  nose straight up or down.
- The magnetic declinations (Hakone −7.5°, Tokyo −7.6°) are single rough values per area
  (`src/areas.ts`).
- The municipal boundaries are thinned to about 50 m; the place is looked up twice a second, and
  within 55 km of the origin no municipality means the sea (`src/hud/flyingOver.ts`).
